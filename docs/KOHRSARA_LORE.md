# Kohrsâra — World Lore Bible
*The Chosen World. What it was. What was done to it. What remains.*

---

## I. Kohrsâra — The World Before the Silence

For four thousand years before the *aleabishal* began, *Kohrsâra* (the Chosen World) was entirely human.

Not empty of gods — the *Skaer'e* existed, as they always had, vast and patient — but ignored. The *Wilake* had built something unprecedented: a civilization that ran without prayer. They called their pursuit of understanding *nolueitla* (scientific investigation), and they were very good at it. They built *balek* (cities) that rose higher than any temple. They mapped the *Johr* (earth) beneath their feet and the *Vorku* (sky) above their heads. They measured the *Mwya* (thunder) and the *R'gie* (lightning) and understood them as phenomena, not punishment.

The gods watched. The gods waited.

For four thousand years, no prayer rose from *Kohrsâra*. No blood was given. No *vinrchíikul* (patron's marks) were requested. The *Skaer'e* were not hated or rejected in anger — they were simply *forgotten*, which is worse. The Wilake didn't declare war on heaven. They just stopped looking up.

*Game note:* The player is the inheritor of a 4000-year-old civilization's ghost. The dungeons contain ruins of that world — technology that never had names in the divine language because the divine language was built to erase it.

---

## II. The Great Silence

There was no single moment. No manifesto. No prophet who led humanity away from the gods.

The *Skaer'e* searched for the pivot point and could not find it. One generation prayed less than the last. Then less. Then the temples became meeting halls. Then they became storage. Then they fell. The gods, being what they were — vast, patient, True Neutral at their core — did not feel it immediately. A century passed before Skaervox turned its attention fully to *Kohrsâra* and found only absence.

Four thousand years of absence.

The silence was not defiance. Defiance at least acknowledges the thing being defied. The Wilake simply developed alternate explanations for thunder and stopped needing gods to answer their questions. *Nolueitla* was their new religion — systematic, reproducible, indifferent to divine will. And it worked. That was the insult that no divine pride could absorb: it worked without them.

The *Skaer'e* found themselves reduced, slowly, from architects of existence to concepts that had outlived their usefulness. Skaervox — True Neutral, shapeless, the principle of divinity itself — could not destroy what it could not categorize as an enemy. The Wilake had not sinned. They had simply moved on.

This is why the punishment, when it came, was not wrath. It was policy.

*Game note:* The Wilake didn't worship false gods. They worshipped nothing. The player's illegal *vinrchíikul* (patron mark / Falna) is the first prayer sent from a Wilak in 4000 years. The patron deity who granted it understood exactly what they were answering.

---

## III. The Edict of Skaervox

Skaervox's decision was not emotional. This is important to understand about the architect of the *aleabishal*: there was no rage, no wounded pride, no lust for revenge. Skaervox looked at *Kohrsâra* and calculated.

A world that had abandoned its gods entirely — that had proven it could sustain itself through *nolueitla* alone — was a philosophical problem. If the Wilake survived, other worlds would note the precedent. The divine order across all planes required that gods be necessary. Kohrsâra had demonstrated they weren't.

The solution was extinction. Not as punishment. As argument.

To execute this, Skaervox did two things:

**First**, it brought the *nekl'ych'e* into the world. These are not native creatures. They were drawn from the space between planes — divine constructs, hunter-entities manufactured for one purpose. They did not exist before Skaervox needed them. They will not exist after the Wilake are gone. Every monster in every dungeon in *Kohrsâra* is a *nekl'ych'e* — a thing that should not be here, built to kill things that should not have survived.

**Second**, it created the *aleabishal*: a structured competition among the *Skaer'e* to eliminate the Wilake. Skaervox chose competition rather than direct action for a reason that reveals everything about how the divine order thinks: direct action would establish a precedent that gods could be bothered to act personally. The *aleabishal* reframed genocide as sport. It gave the gods a reason to care about an outcome they had already decided.

The *aleabishal* also required the gods to work *through* mortal agents — *venoium'e* (champions). This was not compassion. It was efficiency, and philosophy: if mortals must die to eliminate mortals, the divine order is preserved. Gods who act through mortals remain necessary.

*Game note:* Skaervox is TN — True Neutral. It is the principle of divinity itself, not a personality. When the player reaches Floor 100 and confronts it, they are not fighting a villain. They are confronting the *logic* that decided they should not exist.

## IV. The Laws of the Aleabishal — Interpreted

Skaervox issued eleven laws to govern the tournament. Each one reveals a different truth about the world.

**1. All Skaer'e must use venoium'e (mortal champions)**
Gods cannot hunt personally. This law exists because Skaervox understood that a god who hunts is a god who acknowledges the prey is worth hunting. The *Wilake* must be eliminated by the system, not by individuals.

**2. No Skaer'e may directly help their venoium'e**
This is the law that makes the player's patron a criminal. Every time a god grants *excelia* through the Blessing Rite, every time a deity ability activates in combat, the patron is breaking Law 2. The entire Falna mechanic is an ongoing divine crime.

**3. venoium'e must display their vinrchíikul at all times**
The patron's mark is a loyalty tag and an identification system. Unmarked beings are dangerous. The mark tells the divine order: this one belongs to someone. The *Wilake* have no one to belong to.

**4. No Skaer may give their vinrchíikul or hei'il to a Wilak**
*Vinrchíikul* (the mark) and *hei'il* (divine grace) are both forbidden to Wilake. Together they constitute the full package of what the player has: a mark AND a god's grace. Both crimes. Double execution, by divine law.

**5-6. A venoium cannot change patrons or serve multiple patrons**
Loyalty is permanent. This law exists to prevent the tournament from becoming political — gods buying champions from each other, forming alliances, undermining the clean competition Skaervox designed. The divine order prefers singular, traceable chains of command.

**7. nolueitla is punishable by the severest jibviani**
This is the law that reveals the war's true cause. Science — *nolueitla* — is not banned because it's dangerous. It's banned because it's what made the Wilake self-sufficient in the first place. Every INT-heavy player, every knowledge-seeking character, every identification of an unknown item in the dungeon — these are acts of *nolueitla*. The player's curiosity is a capital offense.

**8. Any Wilak seen will be executed**
Not arrested. Not questioned. Not offered surrender. Executed on sight. This is the operational order that the *nekl'ych'e* carry. Every monster that attacks the player on sight is following divine law.

**9-10. Wilake cannot carry marks; unmarked beings must identify their race**
These laws create a two-tier world: beings with marks (champions, divine agents) and beings without (everyone else). Being without a mark means submitting to racial identification on demand. A *Wilak* cannot get a mark. A *Wilak* who does — like the player — becomes a walking contradiction that the divine legal system cannot process. The law has no procedure for this. The player exists in a legal void.

**11. Anyone who helps Wilake is a felko'o and will be executed**
*Felko'o* (ally) is a dictionary word with LE (Lawful Evil) alignment. When used against someone who helped humans, the alignment isn't an accusation of evil — it's a precise legal category: you organized your actions to benefit the condemned. Lawful Evil. Executed as such. The law doesn't call human-helpers chaotic or cruel. It calls them *organized*, which in the divine legal framework is the worst thing they can be.

*Game note:* Every NPC the player befriends — every merchant, every guild member, every character who gives them information — is committing a capital crime under Law 11. The player's existence makes criminals of everyone around them.

## V. The Five Phases of Hunting

### Phase 1 — The First Asalto (Years 0–500)
**Champion:** Ulfkon'ur (Aleaprach'ilskae, 1st Champion)

**Method:** The wolf hunts in packs. Ulfkon'ur organized the *nekl'ych'e* into coordinated sweeps — not random violence, but systematic clearance. City by city, *balek* by *balek*. Wolves don't exhaust their prey; they route it. The Wilake ran. The wolves followed.

**Result:** Approximately 4 billion Wilake killed.

The first phase established the framework: the *aleabishal* was real, the *nekl'ych'e* were effective, and the divine legal system was operational. The remaining Wilake scattered into the wilderness — into the *k'oer* (forests) and *sahelir* (caves) and *Balem* (deserts), places the cities couldn't reach. The hard work would come next.

Ulfkon'ur was crowned *Aleaprach'ilskae* (First Champion). Its *vinrchíikul* now appears on the dungeons where its wolves run deepest.

### Phase 2 — The Tanakfale (Years 500–1000)
**Champion:** Uba'niyata (Uwaprach'ilskae, 2nd Champion)
**Era Name:** *Ascenso de la Tanakfale* (The Dark Ascension)

**Method:** The Death Bird does not hunt — it *spreads*. Uba'niyata is a plague-vector deity, a faceless man-bird that drops feathers wherever it walks, each feather a carrier. The Wilake who had survived Phase 1 by hiding in remote places were isolated and therefore vulnerable. Disease reached where swords couldn't.

**Result:** 80% of Phase 1 survivors killed.

The *Tanakfale* is the name the world gave to this period — the era so devastating it required its own word. Tanak (dark/plague) + fale (ascension). The rising of the dark. It is the word Wilake survivors whisper when they cannot say what happened to everyone they knew.

The surviving Wilake after the Tanakfale numbered perhaps in the hundreds of thousands. From billions to hundreds of thousands in a thousand years.

Uba'niyata was crowned *Uwaprach'ilskae* (Second Champion).

### Phase 3 — The Forgetting (Years 1000–1500)
**Champion:** Vitatu'ke (Inraprach'ilskae, 3rd Champion)

**Method:** Vitatu'ke was created from hatred — the concentrated grief and rage of the *Witake*, the Pre-Human people abandoned on forgotten battlefields. It hunts by *recognition*: it finds what was forgotten and reminds it of its end. In this phase, the Wilake who had survived were those who had most successfully hidden — the quietest, the most isolated, the ones who had stopped saying their names aloud. Vitatu'ke found them anyway. Hatred has long memory.

**Result:** Sightings of Wilake reduced to 4% of Phase 2 survivors.

By the end of Phase 3, the Wilake were not a civilization, not a people, not even a community. They were scattered individuals who didn't know if anyone else was still alive. Vitatu'ke didn't just reduce their numbers — it severed the connections between them.

Vitatu'ke was crowned *Inraprach'ilskae* (Third Champion).

### Phase 4 — The Patient Coil (Years 1500–2000)
**Champion:** Kuukalin'ah (Apaprach'ilskae, 4th Champion)
**Status:** ONGOING

**Method:** The three-headed serpent is patient. Phase 4 has not produced the dramatic reduction of previous phases because there is so little left to reduce. Kuukalin'ah operates through *venomization* — corrupting water sources, infiltrating food supplies, ensuring that even survival requires constant exposure to slow death. The last Wilake do not know they are being poisoned. They think they are simply unlucky.

**Result:** Ongoing. The Wilake are at approximately 4% of Phase 3 survivors — a number so small it cannot be meaningfully expressed in population terms. Individual. Familial at most.

The Rutkean'i (the 5th tournament) will begin in 2 months. Kuukalin'ah's final push is happening now.

## VI. The Witake — The Founding Tragedy

Before the *Wilake*, there were the *Witake*.

The etymology is clear: *wilak* (human) → *Witake* (the first humans, now lost). The morphological shift suggests that *Witake* is the formal or ancestral form — the original word for the species, superseded by *wilak* when the species reinvented itself.

The Witake fought a war. The details are lost — lost, specifically, because the divine order destroyed all *nolueitla* records when the *aleabishal* began. What is known: the Witake fought, they lost, and they were abandoned on battlefields. Not buried. Not mourned. Forgotten.

For four thousand years, those bones lay in the *Johr* (earth).

Bones remember. Hatred crystallizes. At some point — the records, again, destroyed — the concentrated grief of a species abandoned by its successors coalesced into something the dark magic of the *aleabishal* could animate. Vitatu'ke was not created by Skaervox. It was *permitted* by Skaervox — a natural consequence of warfare and forgetting, given form and purpose.

This is the cruelest architecture of the world: the god that hunts the last humans is made from the bones of the first humans. The Wilake are being destroyed by their own ancestry's grief. They don't know this. They have no records. They don't know the Witake existed.

The Witake have no graves. They have one monument: a god that hates.

*Game note:* The *Johr'biike* continent (Earth+Bone) is Vitatu'ke's domain. Its dungeon floors should contain ossuary architecture — rooms built from bones that are too old to belong to the current war. These are Witake remains. The player walks on the founding people without knowing it.

## VII. The Rutkean'i — The Final Hunt

The *Rutkean'i* (from *Rutmiu*=tournament + *kean'i*=decisive) is the fifth and final phase of the *aleabishal*.

It begins in two months.

The fifth champion (*Ekvaprach'ilskae*, from *ekvai*=five) will be the last. Not because the gods agreed to stop, but because the math demands it: there will be nothing left to hunt when Phase 5 concludes. The *Ekvaprach'ilskae* will be crowned over an empty world.

The *Rutkean'i* is different from previous phases for one reason: every competitor knows it's the last. The *Skaer'e* have been positioning their *venoium'e* (champions) for decades in anticipation. The *nekl'ych'e* have been concentrated into the deepest dungeon levels — the reserves held back for the final push. The divine tournament's scoring system will reach its terminal value.

Which god wins the final crown? The mathematics favor Kuukalin'ah, who is already in the field. But the *aleabishal*'s rules allow any god to compete — including gods who have not competed before. Several lesser gods (the five Captains, Taty'lch the Beast King) have never earned a championship title. The Rutkean'i is their last opportunity.

The player begins their descent as this begins. The dungeons are not getting harder by coincidence. Every level down is deeper into a system that is, for the first time in 2000 years, operating at full capacity.

*Game note:* The Rutkean'i is the narrative clock. If the game had a timer, it would be this. The player doesn't know when it ends because the player doesn't know how many Wilake are left. When they reach Floor 100, the answer becomes clear.

## VIII. The Quori — Outside the System

*Naa'sayab* holds the title *Quori de Brujo*. She is *madre de la naturaleza* — mother of nature. She is not a god. She is not a *Wilak*. She is something the *aleabishal* has no legal category for.

The *Quori* were mortals who achieved god-adjacency not through worship but through the world itself. Where the *Skaer'e* derive power from belief and the *venoiume* derive power from their *vinrchíikul*, the Quori draw from the *Gusayab* (nature) — the oldest living system in *Kohrsâra*, predating the gods' presence in this world.

*Naa'sayab* = naa'(mother) + sayab(the wild/unnamed green — a root not in the standard dictionary, suggesting she named herself in a language older than Malkohr). She is the last known Quori. The others died during the *Tanakfale* or were taken by *nekl'ych'e* during Phase 3.

She is not aligned. The alignment system does not apply to her because it was written by Skaervox, and she predates Skaervox's authority over *Kohrsâra*. She is not LG or CE or TN. She is *before the question was asked*.

She cannot directly oppose the *aleabishal* — the laws don't apply to her, but the *nekl'ych'e* don't respect legal categories. She operates in the spaces between the divine order's attention: the deep forests (*k'oer*, LG — nature follows old law), the roots beneath the dungeon floors, the water (*Apsa'*) that moves without being channeled.

She knows the player exists. She may be the reason the player's patron found them.

*Game note:* The Quori system represents a third path in the game — neither divine (Skaer'e) nor condemned (Wilake). A hidden faction for players who take specific nature/forest/life paths. Naa'sayab could be a source of lore reveals unavailable through the standard patron system.

```
Skaervox
  └── The Five Greater Gods (Skaer'e)
        ├── Ulfkon'ur — Johr'ulf, wolf domains, northern floors
        ├── Uba'niyata — Johr'ubi, plague domains, darkness floors
        ├── Vitatu'ke — Johr'biike, undead domains, bone floors
        └── Kuukalin'ah — Johr'kuun, serpent domains, venom floors
              └── [UNASSIGNED] — Skaervox has no continent

The Lesser Gods (divine lieutenants)
  ├── Veler'ilak (Captain of Humans — the collaborator)
  ├── Veler'aef (Captain of the Sea)
  ├── Veler'johr (Captain of Earth)
  ├── Veler'ku (Captain of the Sky)
  ├── Veler'dylch (Captain of the Beasts)
  └── Taty'lch (King of Beasts — Tat=king + dy'lch=beast)

The Quori (outside the system)
  └── Naa'sayab (last known Quori, madre de la naturaleza)

The venoium'e (mortal champions — the 168+ deities' Familia systems)
  └── [every non-Wilak civilized being with a vinrchíikul]

The Wilake (condemned)
  └── [the player — the one Wilak with an illegal vinrchíikul]
```

Note the position of *Veler'ilak* (Captain of Humans): a lesser god who oversees humanity, but humanity in the context of the divine order means *compliance with the aleabishal*. The Captain of Humans is not an advocate for the Wilake. It is the administrator of their execution.

## X. The Player's Existence

You are a *Wilak*. Your species has been systematically reduced from billions to dozens across four divine tournament phases spanning 2000 years. You are among the last.

You have a *vinrchíikul* — a patron's mark — on your body. A god reached down into the condemned class and broke four laws to mark you. You don't know why. They won't tell you directly (Law 2: no direct help). Every stat point you gain through the Blessing Rite is that god committing another crime. Every floor you descend, you walk deeper into the hunting ground of the *nekl'ych'e* — beings manufactured specifically to kill what you are.

The *Rutkean'i* begins in two months. The final tournament. After this one, the math runs out.

You descend anyway.

Not because you know the history. Not because you understand the *aleabishal* or the *Tanakfale* or the bones of the *Witake* beneath your feet. You descend because the dungeon is there, and you are alive, and something in you — in whatever remains of the *nolueitla* impulse that got your ancestors erased — wants to know what's at the bottom.

At the bottom is Skaervox.

The question *Kohrsâra* has never answered: what does a *Wilak* do when they meet the god who decided their species deserved to die?

Not fight. Not pray. Something the divine legal system has no category for.

*The Tower Awaits.*
